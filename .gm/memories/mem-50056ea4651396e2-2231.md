INTERIM - to be superseded by the uniform-direction whole-surface sweep; prune that one in. DO NOT compare the crossing tilt statistic to a route tilt statistic: they are different estimators. The 60 'crossings' each take maxTerrainTiltDeg as the MAX over a 400 m window sampled at 2 m (401 fine samples), so it is an extreme-value statistic; the 16 routes report one tilt per sample at 10 km spacing. Comparing them produced the false reading '28 of 60 crossings have terrain above 45 deg therefore circumnavigation is blocked by terrain generation'. MEASURED 2026-10-02 from the four .gm/circ-*.json reports: over 643 route samples across 16 circumnavigation routes (equator E-W, E-W at +/-15 and +30 deg latitude, N-S over both poles and its antipode, three diagonals, eight corner routes), 14 exceed 45 deg terrain-only tilt = 2.18 pct. Over the 60 crossings, 28 of 60 exceed it = 46.67 pct. The route figure is coarse (10 km spacing misses narrow cliffs) so it understates blocking; the crossing figure is a max over a fine window so it overstates prevalence. They bound the answer from below and above and neither is the planet-wide number - only a uniform-direction sweep at fine spacing settles it. Per route, terrain-above-45 counts out of 41 (or 40 / 29): ew-equator 3, ew-anchor-lat-15 3, ew-anchor-lat-plus-30 2, ns-over-both-poles 0, ns-over-both-poles-antipode 0, diag-incl-45 1, diag-incl-110 0, diag-incl-160 1, and only 4 of 328 across the eight corner routes - so the corner routes themselves are the GENTLEST terrain (max 56.51 deg but 1.2 pct above 45), and the steepest routes are the east-west ones near the equator and anchor latitude.
