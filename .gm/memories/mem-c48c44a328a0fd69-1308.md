The collider initial ring is placement-generation-bound, so budgeting it (deferring like a steady rebuild) makes boot far worse, not better. Measured on tps-game with the real streamers and a real PhysicsWorld, 1000 m player grid, trunk radius 64 cap 384, rock radius 32 cap 128, intervalMs 300: placement generation for the 2705 trunk ring chunks at 64 players costs 6063 ms (2.242 ms/chunk), 96% of the 6289 ms ring; rock is 0.883 ms/chunk over 1163 chunks, all of its 945 ms. COMPUTE_BUDGET_MS is 2.5 ms, so a pass admits about one chunk (and often none once the per-pass ring walk over every centre has eaten into it): a budgeted boot took 981 passes and 35.5 s at 16 players (674 chunks) against 2.95 s unbudgeted, and at 64 players it did not converge in 4001 passes / 200 s (67 of 1594 bodies, 721 of 2705 chunks). Unbudgeted, every player's first collider lands at the end of one stall: 165 ms at 1 player, 3.73 s at 16, 7.26 s at 64 (one contiguous event-loop stall). Budgeted first-collider times are 22 ms to 34.1 s at 16 players and up to 167 s at 64. So do not budget the boot ring; the lever is per-cluster batches (a measured 94.7 ms of placement work per cluster at 64 players), which would put the first batch at roughly 0.8 s and the last at roughly 6.1 s instead of every player at 7.26 s.
