Jolt's three JoltSettings limits are not the same kind of thing, and two of the three fail SILENTLY -- measured 2026-10-06 by replica against a real PhysicsWorld, contradicting the earlier assumption that undersizing aborts the process. (1) maxContactConstraints IS a real cap and degrades without any signal: a replica of 3072 statics plus 512 dynamics rests correctly at Y=-0.22 with 512 but falls through to Y=-49 with 256 -- no assert, no exception, bodies just drop through the ground. (2) maxBodies IS a real cap and fails by silent truncation -- bodies past the limit are simply not created. (3) maxBodyPairs is NOT a cap at all, it is a QUEUE BATCH: a replica holding 5560 concurrent contact manifolds produces bit-identical trajectories and identical manifold counts at maxBodyPairs 8 and at 65536, while still costing exactly 88 B per slot, so it is a pure allocation knob and can be cut freely. Only the Jolt WASM HEAP itself aborts (Aborted(OOM) in new J.JoltInterface, src/physics/World.js:60) -- that is the thing that kills every world in the process, not the per-world limits. So: size maxBodies and maxContactConstraints from a measured peak with real headroom and make overshoot loud, and treat maxBodyPairs as allocation only. Measured census at the largest cluster the config allows (64 collider centres, 512 dynamics, 8 m move + 5 km jump, tps-game r=63600): peak 3587 resident bodies, 329-516 contact manifolds. Sizing chosen 8192/8192/2048, which moved marginal cost 20238432 B (19.301 MiB) -> 12372064 B (11.799 MiB) and CLUSTER_HEAP_WORLD_CEILING 5 -> 8; 10 cluster worlds then ran in one process at 60 ticks/s with 9.4 MiB of the 128 MiB heap free, where 7 used to abort. Allocation sweep reproduced the coefficients exactly: 88.00 B/pair, 33.02 B/body, 336.00 B/constraint.
