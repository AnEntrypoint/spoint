project/netcode-lockstep-profile-agreed-drop-and-pacing: worldDef.netcode.profile 'lockstep' runs src/netcode/LockstepGameLoop.js inside each peer's BrowserServer worker via PeerSimSession, with frames 'wwlockstep:' (LockstepInputTransport input/drop, ConsensusVoter checksum). Rules that bit: (1) the sim must be capped by its own driver clock (simTick < driverTicks); without it both peers race each other's inputs and ran at 86 Hz on a 30 Hz world. (2) Time sync must use the newest received remote tick and an EMA-smoothed advantage; the raw contiguous-confirmed advantage spikes on every retransmit and both peers yielded until the sim ran at 79% speed. (3) Dropping a peer must be agreed: every survivor broadcasts the dropped peer's inputs it holds plus its contiguous 'have'; the cut is the max 'have' over survivors; the dropped peer's inputs are frozen (ignored) once a drop starts, otherwise a survivor simulates past its own report and diverges. (4) Two peers have no majority: DesyncDetector.strictMajority gates ejection and a 1v1 mismatch counts as unattributedDesyncs. The lockstep checksum folds every appRuntime entity position so app sims such as lockstep-rts are covered. scripts/peer-profile-harness.mjs --world=lockstep-rts exercises all of this, including --peers=3 --killPeer / --cheatPeer. Input delay must exceed one-way latency plus jitter in ticks, or the sim slows (75 ms one way with 5% loss needs 6 ticks at 30 Hz).
